How the log is decoded

The bus is CANopen-like at 125 kbit/s, 11-bit IDs. Full decode: docs/protocol.md. Built-in data: the sample (capture 3 excerpt, 2 Oct 2026) and capture 1 (23 Sep 2026, main helm).

IDSourceDecode
0x181 / 0x282Node 1 main helm / node 2 flybridge, 150 msbyte 0 flags; bytes 1 and 4 gear (FF ahead, 00 neutral, 01 astern); bytes 2 and 5 throttle 0 to 100 %. Both heads send their levers all the time, in command or not.
byte 0Both heads40 always set; 01 this head has command; 02 warm-up; 08 synchronisation. Warm-up and sync are system states, mirrored by the other head 150 ms later.
0x384 / 0x485Nodes 4 and 5, actuators, 153.6 msC0 neutral, 4C shifting, 44 ahead engaged, 48 astern engaged, 40 booting
0x7nnHeartbeats00 boot-up (actuators only), 05 operational
  • Which head counts: the head in command has a red border; the gearboxes follow its levers. The other head is dimmed: its levers are sent but ignored.
  • Synchronisation: the head in command writes its right lever into both the port and starboard bytes.
  • Buttons are not on the bus. A press is drawn when its flag bit changes, as a 1.5 s hold ending at that frame (the manual specifies 1.5 s holds). The mirror 150 ms later is not a press.
  • LEDs are reconstructed: Command red when the head has command; Warm/Sync blinking in warm-up, steady in sync; Engine steady when the head has command and that actuator is in neutral.
  • Lever angle is rebuilt from gear and throttle: neutral upright, the gear detent at 20 % of travel, full throttle at the end stop.
  • Port and starboard: bytes 1 and 2 and node 4 are port on the test boat. The swap switch is there for other installations.
  • Rev C firmware logs (capture 1) start with a compressed half second of timestamps; it is shaded on the timeline.
